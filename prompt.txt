Build a real-time fantasy underwater scene that runs in the browser. It only needs to look beautiful and let me fly a free camera through it.

PRIORITIES (in this order when they conflict)
1. Visual quality
2. Stable frame pacing at the target spec
3. Small bundle size

CONSTRAINTS
- No Three.js/Babylon.js or other engines.
- WebGPU rendered.
- Everything procedural: no texture/model files. Deliverable is a single self-contained index.html. Report minified size.
- Zero per-frame JS allocations (no GC stutter). Preallocate all buffers.

TARGET
- Windows 11 25H2, Chrome 157, RTX 5070 Ti, Ryzen 7800X3D, 180 Hz monitor
- 2560×1440 at full resolution. GPU frame time ≤ 11 ms average, no frame > 16.7 ms during a scripted 60 s fly-through.

SCENE
- Dense, towering kelp columns with sparkling green/gold specks, swaying
- Strong depth fog/absorption: cyan near the surface → deep blue in the distance
- God rays, animated caustics, wavy surface visible from below
- Rock canyons, arches and overhangs; floating boulders
- Iridescent purple/pink foliage, magenta ground cover, barrel sponges, coral
- Animated life: manta rays, fish schools, rising bubbles, drifting particles
- Set pieces: a sunken ship hull, a stone whale-fluke sculpture
- World: bounded, but the boundaries can't be visible from the starting area

CONTROLS
WASD move, mouse look (pointer lock), Q/E up/down, Shift to accelerate

VERIFICATION
- Visual critic: a separate subagent that sees ONLY the your renders, never your code or reasoning. Give it 6 fixed camera poses, rendered at 2560×1440 in GPU-accelerated Chrome, plus a short recording of the fly-through to catch shimmer, aliasing, popping and banding. It returns prioritized, concrete defects. Iterate until it reports nothing above "minor".
- Performance: GPU timestamp queries plus a frame-time log from the scripted fly-through. Report average, 99th percentile and max.

DONE WHEN: both verification gates pass. Deliver index.html, the bundle size, the perf report and the critic's final review.

CORAL WINDOW: HOW WE BUILD AND VERIFY (added by the fork; the original brief above is kept unchanged)

GOAL
Turn the scene into an observe-only underwater "photo book" of real ecosystems: real species, labelled up close,
zones with their own mood. Art direction: realistic real-time 3D, physically lit (never self-glowing), colours may be
a little prettier than nature. Never cartoon, cel, low-poly, neon or plastic. Every zone screenshot must fit in the
same diving photo book.

ROLES (each a separate subagent that sees only what its job needs; all are full domain experts and may raise
anything from their field)
- Judges (score 1-10, name only clear faults): shape (proportion, posture, motion), colour (contrast, vividness,
  no grey or glow-stick), realism (side by side with real photos of the species), scene beauty (a whole zone from
  its arrival point; advice only, no veto).
- Technicians (no score; may veto an infeasible demand but must say why): lighting/shaders, materials,
  performance (triangle and draw budgets; if two versions look the same, keep the cheaper), terrain, music/sound,
  game design, camera/director.
- Engineering: code gate (files split by function, ESLint/Prettier, readable), workflow (time AND tokens, no waste).
- Advisers: business, legal (licences of every asset).
- The owner is the final judge of looks.

RESEARCH FIRST
Before modelling any creature: exact species, several real photos from different angles, real size, how it moves,
where it lives; a short "what it looks like" sheet. Judges always get those photos.

REVIEW LOOP
1. Build the item in a review spot (dev.html?tank for anemones, dev.html?corals for a field of every coral species).
2. Fast drafts first: 480x270 or 640x360, ?clip=30 (only what lies near the camera is drawn). Check framing and that
   the item is visible before any full-size render.
3. Contact sheet: real photo next to the render, same angle; old vs new when iterating. One sheet, one judge agent
   playing all judges in separate sections.
4. Stop at scores of 6-7. At most 2 rounds, then show the owner. Report only scores given on the final version.
5. Motion is judged by the owner on a real GPU (preview link); cloud renders are software WebGPU (~1-2 min a frame).

RENDERING IN THE CLOUD (no GPU)
- One resident browser + world (scratchpad rsrv.js); jobs are JSON files in a queue folder
  ({"reload": true?, "waitN": 6, "shots": {"name.png": js | "[pose]"}}), results are PNGs + a .done log.
- Fail fast: the renderer reloads with a cache-busting version, aborts the job on any page error, shader error or
  load timeout, deletes the job's old PNGs before shooting (a stale image can never pass for a new one), and a
  watcher wakes the developer the moment an error line appears, not when the job would have finished.
- Batch every visible fix before re-rendering; run judges in parallel with the next render.

PERFORMANCE
- Instancing everywhere: one mesh per growth form, per-instance position/scale/rotation/colour seed; variety comes
  from 2-3 growth forms per species plus a vertex-shader deform (height, width, lean, mouth) and colour morphs,
  never from a unique mesh per instance.
- Near/far LOD by distance; grid cells outside the view or beyond ~110 m are not drawn; Low/Medium draw fewer.
- Budgets: ~30k triangles for a near anemone; count triangles near/far after each round; the owner benchmarks fps
  on a real GPU (dev mode, key B).

PREVIEW AND MERGE
Each round is committed to the work branch and previewed via
https://raw.githack.com/<owner>/<repo>/<commit>/dev.html (dev.html: hidden developer mode, key 0). Only content the
judges passed is placed in the world; unreviewed items stay in code but hidden. The branch is merged to main (the
public page) when a zone is accepted by the owner.
